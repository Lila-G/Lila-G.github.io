My research is centered on data-driven digital medicine, with machine learning methods tailored to noisy, longitudinal, real-world wearable data.

## Main Themes

### Digital cortisol and stress physiology
I develop models to infer latent endocrine dynamics from wearable signals such as heart rate, heart rate variability, activity, sleep, and contextual features. The objective is to enable continuous, personalized stress assessment outside hospital settings.

### Wearable sensing for clinical outcomes
I design and evaluate computational pipelines that connect consumer and medical-grade wearable measurements to clinically relevant endpoints for prevention and early intervention.

### Signal processing and state-space modeling
I combine interpretable signal processing with modern machine learning to improve robustness, temporal consistency, and clinical interpretability in physiological modeling.

### Previous work
During my PhD, I developed non-invasive machine learning approaches for dysphagia-related pharyngolaryngeal activity detection from high-resolution cervical auscultation sensors. I also worked on dialogue act segmentation and speech technologies in collaborative NLP and health informatics projects.
